# Brief a branding agency

A good brief gets a faster, better-fitted reply and a more accurate fixed price. Work through it with the user in conversation; do not invent answers.

## 1. Is an agency the right move?

Ask what the business is and where it is in its life. A full brand project suits a business with revenue or funding, a real audience, and a moment that matters (a raise, a launch, a rebrand, a move upmarket). For a pre-revenue idea or side project still being tested, a template tool or AI logo generator is usually the better spend. Say so plainly if that is the case.

## 2. Collect the brief

Capture, in the user's own words:

- **Business**: name, what it does, who it serves, one line on why it exists.
- **Stage**: pre-seed, seed, Series A+, or established.
- **The change needed**: what is wrong or missing with the current brand (nothing yet, drifted, outgrown, new product or sub-brand, naming needed).
- **Scope**: strategy, naming, identity, digital or motion design, launch, brand guidelines, or "not sure yet".
- **Audience and competitors**: who they are compared against.
- **Timing**: any fixed date (a raise, launch, event).
- **Budget**: a range or ceiling. Agencies price better when they know it. Fixed-price quotes are normal for this work.
- **Decision-maker** and who else must approve.

## 3. Compare options

If the user wants a shortlist, treat each agency the same: ask for fixed prices, what is owned at the end (logo and asset copyright should be assigned in writing), how many rounds of feedback are included, and who actually does the work.
